## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Aisha, 27, casual viewer: “I just re-watch the same four comfort shows. Discovering anything new feels like a chore, so I don’t.” Used as the closest proxy for the Wanderer, because no interview directly sampled the low-engagement, trending-fed segment. The friction is passive disengagement: discovery costs effort, so the user stops trying and defaults to the familiar. Flag: this is a proxy, not a direct match, and the research gap is itself a finding.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Wanderers are 41% of the base, carry the lowest LTV ($8.40) and fewest sessions (1.1/week), watch only 18% curated content (61% trending), yet churn drops 22 points when exposed to Spotlight — the largest treatment effect of any segment, versus 14 points for Casuals. Reinforced by Snapshot 2: Spotlight cohorts retain 12 to 15 points higher at Month 1 and decay slower (Dec holds 52% at Month 3 vs ~39% for full-library cohorts).
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** Role: a high-volume-of-the-base but low-engagement viewer who opens StreamLine out of habit, drifts through a trending feed, and rarely commits to anything worth staying for. Goal: to land on something worth watching without the effort of hunting for it. Confirmed friction: discovery feels like work, so they don’t do it — they default to trending or the same few familiar titles, engage shallowly (1.1 sessions/week), and churn quietly. The reconciliation steps confirmed this is the segment Spotlight actually moves, even though the original persona work targeted the opposite (curation-hungry) user.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** StreamLine makes discovery effortful enough that its largest, lowest-engagement segment opts out of it entirely, defaulting to a shallow trending feed and churning before the product ever proves its worth.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Expected behaviour change: Wanderers shift from passive trending consumption toward curated content they actually finish, raising session depth and frequency. Maps to retention and revenue: higher early engagement reduces the Month 0→1 leak (the largest in every cohort), and a 22-point churn improvement across 41% of the base is the single biggest absolute churn reduction available. Even at $8.40 LTV, reach × effect size makes this the largest retained-revenue opportunity in the base. Churn down, retention curve flatter, lifetime revenue up through longer tenure rather than higher per-user price.
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Curated-content engagement rate among exposed Wanderers — specifically, the share of Wanderer sessions that start a curated title and reach a meaningful watch threshold (say 20+ minutes). This is the leading indicator that the discovery gap is closing before churn numbers mature. Secondary leading signal: Wanderer sessions per week moving off the 1.1 floor.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Power User retention and session length must not drop. Power Users are 22% of the base, the highest LTV ($14.20), already low-churn, and already 58% curated. If reorienting Spotlight toward Wanderers dilutes the curation quality that retains Power Users, you win the cheap segment and bleed the valuable one. Protect their session length and curated-content satisfaction above all. Secondary guardrail: overall app session-completion rate, so a Wanderer-driven redesign doesn’t worsen the mid-session drop-off seen in Snapshot 1.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Two cuts. (1) Minimum threshold to proceed: within 6 to 8 weeks, exposed Wanderers must show a measurable lift in curated-engagement rate over a matched non-exposed control — if there’s no leading-indicator movement, pause and re-examine before spending more. (2) Go/no-go to scale: after the Dec through Feb Spotlight cohorts reach Month 5 to 6 (two more months of data), confirming the flatter retention curve holds and isn’t an early-cohort artifact. Kill condition: if the guardrail (Power User retention) drops more than a small margin, or if Wanderer churn improvement shrinks below Casuals’ 14 points once exposure is properly controlled rather than self-selected.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on Aisha’s qualitative signal that discovery feels like a chore her segment opts out of, and the Snapshot 3 finding that Wanderers churn 22 points lower when exposed to Spotlight (the largest effect of any segment, across 41% of the base), I believe that removing the effort of discovery for Wanderers — surfacing curated content they’ll actually finish instead of a passive trending feed — will reduce their churn and flatten the Month 0→1 retention leak, as measured by a lift in curated-engagement rate among exposed Wanderers within 6 to 8 weeks and a sustained double-digit churn improvement over a matched control. I will protect Power User retention and session length as the guardrail, and I will make a go/no-go decision to scale once the Spotlight cohorts reach Month 5 to 6 and confirm the flatter retention curve holds.
